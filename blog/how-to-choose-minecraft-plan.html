How to Choose the Perfect Minecraft Hosting Plan

RAM, player slots, modpack support — picking the wrong plan wastes money. Here is exactly what to look for at every budget.

PB
Pushkar Budha · XyleHosting

Why plan choice matters more than you think

Most players pick a Minecraft server plan based on price alone — and then spend hours troubleshooting lag, crashes, and disconnects. The right plan is determined by three things: player count, game mode, and whether you are running mods. Getting any one of these wrong means you are either overpaying or constantly fighting performance issues.

Step 1: Estimate your player count

RAM is allocated per active player. Use these benchmarks as your starting point:

PlayersRecommended RAMServer type
1–5 (private)2–3 GBVanilla / lightweight
5–154–6 GBPaper / Spigot with plugins
15–306–10 GBPaper + heavy plugins or light mods
30–6010–16 GBPaper or modded (Forge/Fabric)
60+16 GB+Dedicated or multi-node setup
Always buy 1–2 GB more RAM than you think you need. Minecraft's Java garbage collector performs much better with headroom. An over-provisioned server costs a few dollars more — an under-provisioned one crashes at peak hours.

Step 2: Decide on your server software

Your server software affects how efficiently RAM and CPU are used:

  • Vanilla — Official Mojang server. Simple but inefficient. Only use for 1–5 players.
  • Paper — Best for most survival/SMP servers. Up to 40% better performance than vanilla.
  • Purpur — Paper fork with extra configuration. Great for 10–50 player servers.
  • Forge — Required for most mods. RAM-heavy. Add 2–4 GB on top of your base estimate.
  • Fabric — Lighter mod loader. Better performance than Forge for same mod count.

Step 3: Account for modpacks

Modpacks are the biggest variable. A 50-mod Fabric pack and a 300-mod Forge pack both have "mods" but need wildly different specs.

  • Light modpack (under 50 mods): Add 2 GB to your base player RAM estimate
  • Medium modpack (50–150 mods): Add 4 GB
  • Heavy modpack (150+ mods, e.g. ATM, FTB): Add 6–8 GB — and prioritise CPU clock speed over core count
Example: Running a 100-mod Forge pack for 20 players? Start at 6 GB (for 20 players on Paper) + 4 GB (medium modpack) = 10 GB minimum.

Step 4: Choose the right CPU tier

Minecraft is almost entirely single-threaded. This means a server with two fast cores outperforms one with eight slow cores. When comparing plans, look for:

  • High single-core clock speed (3.8 GHz+ is ideal)
  • AMD Ryzen 9 or Intel Xeon Gold — both are excellent for Minecraft
  • NVMe SSD storage — chunk loading speed directly affects player experience

Step 5: Pick your location

Latency between your players and the server has a huge impact on gameplay feel. As a rule of thumb:

  • Players in South Asia / Nepal: choose our Nepal node
  • Players in India: choose our India node
  • Players in Southeast Asia: choose our Singapore node
  • Players in Europe: choose our Germany node

Quick decision guide

  • Friends-only private server, vanilla or Paper → Budget Plan (4 GB)
  • Small community, light plugins, 10–20 players → Budget Plan (6 GB)
  • Public SMP, 20–40 players with plugins → Premium Plan (8–12 GB)
  • Modded server (Forge/Fabric), 10–30 players → Premium Plan (10–16 GB)
  • Network with BungeeCord/Velocity proxy → Proxy Plan + individual game servers
